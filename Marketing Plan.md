# Marketing Plan: Gulf Coast Home Maintenance

## TL;DR
- **Lean hard into the one thing no competitor owns: a hyper-regional, Gulf-Coast-specific home-maintenance-and-storm system with an authentic first-time-buyer voice ("Chad").** The Etsy market for generic home maintenance printables is saturated and priced at $2–$13, but no competitor is branded for the Gulf Coast, and none combines seasonal maintenance + hurricane insurance-claim documentation the way these three products do. That regional white space is the whole strategy.
- **Fix the funnel first, then pay for traffic.** Priorities in order: (1) Etsy SEO + a listing video + reviews, (2) Pinterest (the single best free channel for printables), (3) an email list fed by the free phone calendars and a one-page lead magnet, and only then (4) the ~$100/month Meta budget, which should mostly retarget warm traffic rather than chase cold buyers.
- **Sell the products for what they are: the maintenance kit ($12.99) is the volume/gift driver, the storm binder ($16.99) is the seasonal-urgency and highest-value consumer product, and the agent edition ($39) is the B2B repeat-revenue engine.** Push the binder Aug–Oct (peak season now), the maintenance kit and agent edition into the spring homebuying and Q4 gift peaks.

## Key Findings

**The three products (confirmed live on gulfcoasthomemaintenance.com, Aug 2026):**
1. **The Maintenance Kit — $12.99.** 27-page undated printable (two PDFs), 12 months at three effort tiers ("Must Do / Should Do / Going Above," 36 tasks total), the 17-item "Big Ticket Watch List" with coastal-adjusted lifespans, seven house-specific sections, a "how to date everything" page, and a first-month checklist. Etsy listing titled "New Homeowner Gift: Undated Gulf Coast." Licensed for one household.
2. **The Storm Season Binder — $16.99.** 33-page undated fillable PDF (two PDFs, 1,876 fillable fields): insurance policies/deductibles, nine room-by-room inventory sheets, water & supply calculator, a 7-day hurricane countdown, shutdown/evacuation/re-entry sequences, damage log (with a cause column because wind and flood are different policies), and a claim call log. Etsy listing: "Hurricane Prep Binder Printable."
3. **The Agent Edition — $39.00.** Four PDFs: the 27-page kit plus a 4-page closing-table "leave-behind," each in print and fillable versions, branded with a realtor's name/brokerage on every page, licensed to print for every client they close. Etsy listing: "Realtor Closing Gift: Branded Gulf Coast." (Note: this is the confirmed third product — not a spreadsheet workbook. The site also offers three free phone-calendar subscriptions and a "borrowed time" calculator as top-of-funnel tools.)

**Competitive landscape:**
- **Generic home-maintenance printables on Etsy are abundant and cheap.** Benchmarks: PrettySimplePages "Ultimate House Maintenance Planner" (50+ pages) at $7.98 with 829 favorites and Star Seller status; a 16-page checklist/planner; RunawayShea's PP-22 planner (4.8+ rating); listings clustering between roughly $2 and $13, frequently discounted 30–75%. Higher-end multi-part planners reach $13–$15.
- **Hurricane/emergency prep printables** range from ~$3 budget checklists to $18–$19 full binders. Closest thematic matches found: DesignPrintBySofia "Family Storm Emergency Binder…Insurance Claim Organizer" ($18.95) and ProvisionsDepot "Hurricane Prep Checklist Bundle" ($18.00). Fullest-featured true competitors that combine prep + inventory + insurance/claim logs are priced *below* $16.99: DownloadSuite's "Family Emergency Binder" (35 pages + spreadsheet, $6.59) and a Gumroad "Home Inventory and Emergency Preparedness Planner" (50+ pages, $11.99); Printable Pages by CJY's 61-page insurance companion runs $17.99 (10 reviews). Larger 76–80+ page "emergency preparedness bundles" run ~$12–$25.
- **Critical gap: no competitor is branded for the Gulf Coast, and none ties maintenance timing to the region's insurance deadlines (the 30-day flood-policy wait, percentage wind deductibles).** This is genuine white space.
- **Apps are the indirect competition** (HomeZada $59–$99/yr, HomeBinder, Dwellin, Homer, Upkeepify $5–$9/mo). They're subscriptions; the printables win on one-time price, no-login simplicity, giftability, and the ability to physically hold a binder during a power outage. Note: Centriq, a popular app that won the National Association of Home Builders' "Game Changer" award, shut down its consumer product (widely reported as January 31, 2025, though the exact year is disputed), leaving orphaned users.
- **Free resources** (FEMA/Ready.gov, Red Cross, state insurance departments, insurer blogs) are the true "free alternative." They're authoritative but generic, scattered, non-fillable, and not tailored to the Gulf Coast homeowner's calendar. The paid products' value is curation, regional specificity, and a fill-in-once system.

**Channel realities:**
- **Etsy SEO in 2026** rewards conversion rate and click-through, not just keywords: all 13 tags filled with buyer-language variations (each tag ≤20 characters), primary phrase first in the title, a listing video (Etsy prioritizes listings with video), and reviews that contain keywords.
- **Pinterest is repeatedly cited as the #1 free traffic source for printables** — a visual search engine where pins stay discoverable for months/years. Requires a business account, rich pins, vertical 2:3 images, keyworded pin filenames/titles.
- **Meta ads on ~$100/month** ($3.30/day) is below the threshold most guides consider "meaningful" for cold conversion campaigns; the practical play is cheap objectives (traffic/engagement) and retargeting warm audiences. Facebook CPC averages $0.70 for traffic campaigns (per WordStream's 2025 benchmarks), but the "Home and Home Improvement" vertical runs higher at $0.99 CPC; median CPM is around $13.48.
- **The viral proof-of-concept exists:** Kyshawn Lane's "Weekly Home Check" — a first-time homeowner and the first in his family to buy — reached 1 million followers in just six months and now has over 2 million, on the back of a free "52-Week Home Checklist" plus weekly email reminders. He was named to Forbes' 2025 inaugural "Home Improvement 50" as a top creator. This is the exact authentic-story + free-lead-magnet model available to "Chad."
- **2026 hurricane season is forecast below-normal by NOAA.** NOAA's August 6, 2026 updated outlook raised the probability of a below-normal season from 55% to 75%, predicting 7–13 named storms, 2–6 hurricanes, and 0–2 major hurricanes. This slightly softens demand urgency but does not eliminate it — "it only takes one."

## Details

### 1. Competitive Analysis

**Direct printable competitors (maintenance):** The category leaders are generalist, aesthetically-driven planners sold at low prices with heavy discounting. PrettySimplePages (Star Seller, 829 favorites on one listing, ~$7.98) exemplifies the winning generic formula: 50+ pages, botanical/neutral design, "ultimate" framing, bundled renovation/decluttering pages. These shops win on volume, design polish, and review count (hundreds to thousands). They lose on specificity — they assume a climate the buyer may not live in, which is exactly the wedge the site's own copy names ("maintenance advice written for a climate you don't live in").

**Direct competitors (storm/insurance binders):** This sub-niche is thinner and more fragmented. Most listings are *either* prep checklists ($3–$5) *or* home-inventory/insurance trackers, and the few that combine both (DownloadSuite $6.59; Gumroad noyanlimited $11.99; Printable Pages by CJY 61-page insurance companion, $17.99, 10 reviews) are generic. Storm-specific bundles that mention insurance-claim organizing (DesignPrintBySofia $18.95; ProvisionsDepot $18.00) validate a $17–$19 ceiling. **Takeaway: $16.99 is well-supported — it undercuts the closest storm-branded matches while sitting above budget checklists. But the fullest-featured generic competitors are cheaper, so the binder must win on regional specificity and the claim-documentation depth (1,876 fillable fields, damage-cause column, deductible math), not price.**

**Indirect competitors (apps):** HomeZada ($59–$99/yr), Homer, Dwellin, HomeBinder, Upkeepify ($5–$9/mo). They offer reminders, recall alerts, and inventory but require ongoing subscriptions and logins. The printables' counter-positioning: one-time purchase, no subscription, works during a power outage, giftable, and undated so it never expires.

**Free competitors:** FEMA/Ready.gov, Red Cross, state emergency-management and insurance departments. Authoritative but generic and non-actionable as a personal system. Positioning response: "The government tells you *what* to do; this tells you *when*, for *your* coast, and gives you the sheet to fill in while it's calm."

**Gaps to exploit:**
- No Gulf-Coast (or any US-region) branded competitor.
- No competitor ties maintenance to insurance deadlines (May-1 flood-policy logic).
- Generic products don't address salt-air corrosion, Formosan termites, or nine-month A/C runtime — all named on the site.
- The realtor-branded closing-gift angle for a *digital, license-to-print* maintenance product is largely unserved (most realtor closing gifts are physical: cutting boards, signs, candles).

### 2. Positioning Strategy

**Master positioning:** *"The only home-maintenance and storm system built for the Gulf Coast, by someone who bought his first house here."* Three reinforcing pillars — regional specificity, authentic first-time-buyer founder story, and giftability.

**Per-product value propositions:**
- **Maintenance Kit ($12.99):** *"Your house is on a different schedule than the internet thinks."* Lead with the regional-climate hook and the three-tier "don't drown in year one" structure. Secondary angle: the best-value housewarming/closing gift for a Gulf Coast buyer — practical, keepsake, under $15.
- **Storm Season Binder ($16.99):** *"The part of a hurricane nobody prepares for: proving what you owned, and getting paid for it."* Lead with the financial-stakes framing (percentage deductibles, wind vs. flood, contents coverage). This is the urgency/seasonal product and the one with the strongest emotional-and-financial hook.
- **Agent Edition ($39):** *"Twenty closings at twenty-seven pages is 540 sheets — nobody does that twice. This is the closing gift that keeps arriving."* Sell realtors on repeat-use licensing, branding on every page, and the four-page leave-behind that points clients to the free calendars (keeping the agent top-of-mind for referrals).

**Messaging guardrails:** Keep the honest, plain-spoken "Chad" voice — it is a differentiator against templated competitor copy. Avoid over-promising ("guarantees a payout"); the site's own disclaimer language (guidance, not a substitute for a licensed inspector or your policy) should carry into all marketing.

### 3. Channel-by-Channel Plan

**Etsy SEO (foundation — do first):**
- **Titles:** front-load the highest-intent buyer phrase, then stack variations. E.g., Maintenance Kit: "New Homeowner Gift, Gulf Coast Home Maintenance Checklist, Undated Printable Planner, First Time Home Buyer, Housewarming Gift." Binder: "Hurricane Prep Binder Printable, Home Inventory for Insurance Claim, Storm Season Emergency Planner, Gulf Coast, Fillable PDF."
- **Tags (all 13, ≤20 chars, no title-duplication):** e.g., for the binder — `hurricane prep`, `home inventory`, `insurance claim log`, `emergency binder`, `storm season`, `evacuation plan`, `disaster planner`, `fillable pdf`, `hurricane checklist`, `gulf coast`, `flood prep`, `new homeowner`, `prepper binder`. For the kit — `home maintenance`, `new homeowner gift`, `maintenance planner`, `first time buyer`, `housewarming gift`, `home checklist`, `undated planner`, `printable planner`, `gulf coast`, `closing gift`, `home management`, `house binder`, `homeowner gift`.
- **Conversion levers:** add a 15-second listing video (page flip-through), make the first mockup answer "what's included?" at a glance, and treat the description's first two lines as ad copy. Include commercial-use/license terms and print guidance to prevent bad reviews.
- **Reviews:** add a polite "message to buyers" and a PDF insert asking buyers to review and describe *what they used it for* (keyword-rich reviews rank). Reviews are the biggest early lever for a shop opened Aug 14, 2026.
- **Offsite Ads:** under $10k/year the shop can opt out; given a low average order value the fee (15%) is worth watching, but for these price points ($12.99–$39) offsite ads are defensible — keep enrolled initially, monitor the Advertising dashboard monthly, and reassess.
- **Cross-linking:** link the three listings to each other ("pairs with…") to lift average order value.

**Website role:** The site is already excellent as a top-of-funnel and trust asset (free calendars, the "borrowed time" calculator, the founder story). Its jobs: (a) capture emails via the "Hear about the next one" form and the calculator, (b) route buyers to Etsy (where reviews/trust accrue), and (c) host the free lead magnets and blog/guides for SEO and Pinterest landing. Add a lightweight blog/guide section ("What's on the calendar" already exists) to capture Google search for "Gulf Coast home maintenance," "when to buy flood insurance Mississippi," etc.

**Meta/Facebook ads (~$100/month = ~$3.30/day):**
- **Reality check:** this budget is too thin for cold-conversion campaigns to exit Meta's learning phase. Use it for (1) retargeting website visitors and video-viewers/email openers with the binder and kit, and (2) cheap traffic/engagement objectives to build a retargeting pool.
- **Structure:** one campaign, one or two ad sets, consolidated budget (Advantage+ style). Don't spread $3/day across many ad sets.
- **Audiences:** warm first — website visitors (install the Meta pixel), the free-calendar/email list (uploaded custom audience), and engagers. For any cold spend, geo-target the Gulf Coast (Texas→Florida coastal counties) + interests (new homeowners, first-time buyers, hurricane preparedness, Zillow/realtor.com engagers).
- **Creative angles:** (1) the deductible shock ("On a $400k home a 2% wind deductible is $8,000 before the policy pays a cent"); (2) the "written down while it's calm" fill-in demo; (3) the first-time-buyer founder story ("I bought my first house at 45; nobody hands you a manual"); (4) the gift angle for closings/housewarmings.
- **Seasonal timing:** concentrate binder spend now through October (peak season) and again pre-season (April–May, the real insurance deadline). Shift to the maintenance kit and gift angles in the spring buying season and Q4 gift months.
- **Benchmark to change plan:** target cost-per-purchase at/below ~$5–$6 on a $16.99 product (≈3x ROAS after fees). If cold spend can't beat that, put 100% of budget into retargeting.

**Organic social:**
- **Pinterest (highest priority):** business account, rich pins, 10–20+ vertical pins per product with different hooks (problem/solution "value-first" pins), keyworded titles/filenames ("Gulf Coast hurricane prep binder," "first home maintenance checklist"). Boards: "First-Time Homeowner Tips," "Hurricane Prep," "Gulf Coast Living," "Home Maintenance by Season." Pins link to the site (to capture email) or straight to Etsy.
- **TikTok/Instagram Reels (brand-building):** replicate the proven Kyshawn Lane / "dad advice" formula — short, useful, single-tip videos ("the one thing to check on your A/C in August on the coast," "why May is the real hurricane deadline"). Authentic phone-shot content outperforms polished. Use hashtags #firsttimehomeowner #homemaintenance #hurricaneprep #gulfcoast. Drive to the free calendars.
- **Facebook groups:** participate (don't spam) in Mississippi Gulf Coast / Gulfport / Long Beach community groups and homeowner/insurance groups; share the free calculator and calendars as genuinely helpful resources.

**Email list (the fan-base engine):**
- Use a free-tier ESP (e.g., MailerLite or Flodesk free trial; Klaviyo free up to 250 contacts) integrated with the site form and the calculator.
- Feed it with: the free phone calendars (already a no-signup asset — add an optional email capture), a one-page free lead magnet (see §6), and a post-purchase PDF insert.
- Cadence: a welcome sequence + occasional value emails ("this month on the Gulf Coast, check…"), matching the site's honest "only when there's something new" promise. Email revenue avoids Etsy/offsite fees and is the launch list for future products.

**Partnerships:**
- **Realtors** are the highest-leverage partner (they buy the $39 agent edition *and* refer buyers). Target the Gulf Coast Association of REALTORS — which has more than 2,200 REALTOR® and affiliate members across the Mississippi Gulf Coast (141 DeBuys Rd, Gulfport) — and local brokerages (e.g., Coastal Realty Group). Offer a sample leave-behind and a bulk/gift-license pitch.
- **Insurance agents & independent adjusters:** natural co-marketers for the binder ("give your clients the claim-ready binder"). 
- **Home inspectors:** hand the kit/binder to buyers post-inspection (mirrors how HomeBinder is distributed by inspectors).
- **Local angle:** Home Builders Association MS Coast, closing attorneys/title companies, and local hardware stores.

### 4. Seasonal Calendar

- **Now–October (peak hurricane season):** All-in on the Storm Season Binder — Pinterest, Reels, Meta retargeting, group posts. Urgency messaging (season is live).
- **November–December (Q4 gift peak):** Pivot to the Maintenance Kit and Agent Edition as closing/housewarming gifts; run "gift for the new homeowner" pins and ads. Home sales close year-round but gift-buying peaks.
- **January–February:** "New year, new house" + late-winter listing uptick (Feb is now a strong home-sale month). Push the kit's undated angle ("never goes on sale in January because it never expires").
- **April–May (pre-season + spring buying peak):** The most important window — spring is peak homebuying (May carries the highest seller premium, ~13.1%) *and* May is the real insurance deadline. Push all three products; this is when to concentrate ad spend and realtor outreach ahead of closings.
- **June–August (early season + summer closings):** Sustain binder + kit; summer is peak closing season for the gift/agent angle.

### 5. Pricing & Bundling

- **Hold current prices** — they're well-calibrated. $12.99 sits above generic $7–$8 planners (justified by regional specificity) but below premium bundles; $16.99 undercuts storm-binder competitors ($18–$19) while beating them on depth; $39 is strong B2B value.
- **Add a two-product consumer bundle: Kit + Binder at ~$24.99** (vs. $29.98 separately — a ~$5/17% saving). This raises average order value, which matters against Etsy/offsite fees, and gives the "complete first-year system" gift option. Create it as a third consumer listing to capture more search real estate.
- **Consider an introductory review-builder:** a modest launch discount (e.g., 10–15%) on the kit for the first 4–8 weeks to accelerate first sales and reviews, then return to full price (consistent with the undated "never on sale" brand — frame as a "launch" not a recurring sale).
- **Agent edition:** consider a future tiered add-on (e.g., "done-for-you branding" is already offered free — keep it as a differentiator; a premium tier could add co-branded social graphics).

### 6. Fan-Base / Audience Growth

- **Lead magnet:** a genuinely useful one-page free PDF — e.g., "The 12 Must-Do Gulf Coast Home Tasks (One Page)" or "The 7-Day Hurricane Countdown, One Page" — gated by email (or given free with an email opt-in prompt). This is the top-of-funnel that turns the large free-calendar audience into an owned list.
- **The free calendars are a strategic asset** — very few competitors give away a genuinely useful recurring product. Add optional email capture and use them as the primary Pinterest/Reels call-to-action.
- **Community:** build a light identity ("Gulf Coast homeowners who'd rather fix it before it breaks"). Encourage replies/feedback (the site already invites "tell me if anything's wrong") — this participatory voice builds loyalty and product ideas.
- **Content flywheel:** each maintenance task = one Reel + one Pin + one email tip. Repurpose relentlessly. Kyshawn Lane's precedent (1M followers in six months, now 2M+, from a free 52-week checklist + weekly emails) is the template.
- **Reviews & UGC:** ask buyers to share photos of their printed binders; feature them.

### 7. Future Growth (distinct, later focus)

Once the current three products have traction (steady reviews, a growing list), expand along three axes:
- **Other US regions:** the model is inherently replicable — "Southwest Desert" (heat, monsoon, dust), "Upper Midwest" (freeze, ice dams, snow load), "Pacific Northwest" (rain, moss, wildfire smoke), "Tornado Alley," "Northeast" (freeze, nor'easters). Each is a new calendar + regional storm/hazard binder using the same three-tier engine. The owner already plans this.
- **A spreadsheet/workbook line:** a "Home Systems Register & Replacement Fund Forecaster" (Google Sheets/Excel) that turns the Big-Ticket Watch List into an automated savings forecaster — a natural upsell and a different buyer (spreadsheet lovers). This fits the brand and appears in the market as a proven format (e.g., "Cleaning Schedule Spreadsheet for Google Sheets" is a bestselling Etsy category).
- **Other monetization:** the agent edition can expand to insurance agencies and home inspectors (co-branded, bulk licensing); a low-cost printed/POD version via a print partner for buyers who don't want to print; affiliate content on the blog (generators, shutters, dehumidifiers) once traffic justifies it; and eventually a modest paid membership or annual "updated pack." Keep these secondary until the core three are proven.

## Recommendations (Prioritized 30/60/90)

**First 30 days (fix the funnel, cost ≈ $0–$20):**
1. Optimize all three Etsy listings: keyword-front-loaded titles, all 13 tags, a 15-second listing video each, and a "what's included" first image. Cross-link the listings.
2. Add the post-purchase review request (message to buyers + PDF insert).
3. Stand up Pinterest (business account, rich pins) and publish 15–20 keyworded vertical pins across the three products; pin daily.
4. Turn on email capture on the site (free ESP), wire it to the calculator and free-calendar CTAs, and write a 3-email welcome sequence.
5. Create the one-page free lead magnet.
6. Launch a ~15% launch discount on the kit to seed reviews.
*Benchmark to advance:* first 5–10 reviews and Pinterest driving measurable Etsy clicks.

**Days 31–60 (build warm audience, start light paid):**
1. Begin posting 3×/week on Reels/TikTok using the single-tip formula; repurpose to Pins.
2. Install the Meta pixel; start a $3/day retargeting campaign to site visitors + email list (binder-focused while in season).
3. Launch the Kit+Binder bundle listing at $24.99.
4. Begin realtor outreach: DM/email 10–20 local agents and the Gulf Coast Association of REALTORS with a sample leave-behind and the $39 pitch.
5. Post the free calculator/calendars in 2–3 Gulf Coast Facebook groups (as help, not spam).
*Benchmark:* retargeting cost-per-purchase ≤ ~$6; email list into the low hundreds; first agent-edition sale.

**Days 61–90 (scale what works, plan next product):**
1. Double down on the top-performing channel (likely Pinterest or Reels); prune the rest.
2. Shift ad creative toward Q4 gift angles as the season winds down.
3. Approach insurance agents and home inspectors for co-marketing.
4. Decide the next product (most likely the spreadsheet workbook or a second region) based on email-survey feedback and Etsy search data.
*Benchmark:* consistent weekly sales, ≥20–30 reviews across listings, an email list of 300–500, and a repeatable content cadence — the green light to build product #4.

**Thresholds that change the plan:**
- If Etsy Ads/offsite spend exceeds the revenue it drives for two consecutive months → cut daily budget or opt out of offsite ads.
- If Meta retargeting can't hit ~3x ROAS → move 100% of the $100 to boosting top Pins or Etsy Ads instead.
- If the agent edition outsells consumer products per-listing → reallocate outreach time toward B2B (brokerages, insurers) and build the co-branded tier.

## Caveats
- **The Etsy shop is brand-new (opened Aug 14, 2026)** with essentially no review history; early rankings and conversion will be weak until reviews accumulate. Expect a 4–12 week ramp; SEO/tag changes take 2–4 weeks to affect rankings and 1–3 months for new-listing maturity.
- **2026 is forecast a below-normal hurricane season (NOAA raised the probability to 75% on Aug 6, 2026),** which may modestly dampen storm-binder urgency versus an active-season year — but the product is undated and the "it only takes one" message keeps it evergreen; don't over-index on this single season.
- **Competitor review counts and some prices were partly obscured** by Etsy rate-limiting during research; treat specific competitor figures as directionally accurate benchmarks, not exact current values.
- **The ~$100/month Meta budget is below what most 2026 guides consider sufficient for cold conversion campaigns;** results will depend heavily on warm retargeting and organic reach. Manage expectations — paid is the amplifier, not the engine.
- **Solo-operator bandwidth is the real constraint.** The plan is sequenced so that free, compounding channels (Etsy SEO, Pinterest, email) come first; do not attempt all channels at once. Consistency on one or two channels beats sporadic effort across five.
- **Prices, forecasts, and platform fees are current as of August 2026** and will change; revisit quarterly.
