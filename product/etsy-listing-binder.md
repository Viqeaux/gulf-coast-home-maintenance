## The one thing that makes this listing different: it has a clock

Every other listing in this shop sells at the same rate in February as in
September. This one does not, and the whole strategy follows from that.

**Peak of Atlantic hurricane season is around September 10.** Search volume for
every term in the Tags section below is close to nothing in winter and enormous
for the six weeks around that date, then gone again.

**Publish before the demand, not during it.** A listing has no sales history,
no reviews and no ranking signal on its first day, and Etsy will not float a
brand new listing to the top of a term that suddenly has ten thousand searchers
on it. A listing that has been quietly live for four weeks, with a few sales
behind it, is the one that catches the spike. Publishing the day a storm is
named is the single most expensive mistake available here.

**There is no Marketplace Insights data for these terms yet.** The table in
[HANDOFF.md](../HANDOFF.md) covers gift and maintenance terms, not storm terms,
so every keyword choice below is reasoned rather than measured. **Run the tag
list through Insights before publishing**, and if the numbers disagree with the
reasoning, believe the numbers. That check takes ten minutes and it is the
highest value ten minutes available before launch.

**Half the tags are deliberately not about hurricanes**, which is the hedge
against all of the above. `home inventory`, `important documents`, `insurance
claim` and `emergency binder` are searched all year by people who have just
bought a house, just been burgled, or just been told by their agent to make a
list. That is what keeps the listing alive from November to May.

## Listing type

**Digital, instant download.** Answer *yes* to "Is this a digital file?" during

**No custom options and no variations**, for the same reason the realtor

## Title

*140 characters max, 14 words or fewer, no repeated words. This is 99 and 14.*

```
Hurricane Prep Binder: Printable Home Inventory, Insurance Claim Log and Gulf Coast Storm Checklist
```

Search results truncate around 60 characters, so what most people see is
`Hurricane Prep Binder: Printable Home Inventory, Insuranc...`. The panic term
lands first, the two evergreen terms land before the cut, and `Gulf Coast` sits
past it on purpose: Etsy still matches the words, but they do not spend any of
the sixty characters a shopper actually reads.

**On leaving the region out of the visible half.** The kit leads with the
region because it is competing against generic maintenance checklists, where
being Gulf specific is the entire differentiator. Hurricane prep is different:
somebody in Florida or the Carolinas has the same problem and most of this
binder, the inventory, the claim log, the contractor vetting, works anywhere a
storm goes. The Gulf detail is what makes it credible rather than what limits
it, so it belongs in the description and the tags rather than in the first
sixty characters. If Insights shows `gulf coast hurricane` doing real volume,
reverse this.

## Description

Etsy shows only the first few lines before "expand", so the opening two
paragraphs carry the whole pitch and have to stand alone.

```
Your hurricane deductible is probably not a flat dollar amount. It is a percentage of what your house is insured for, and on a $400,000 home a 2 percent wind deductible is $8,000 out of your pocket before the policy pays anything at all. Most people learn that from an adjuster, afterward.

This is a 33 page printable binder for coastal homeowners. It holds the things you can only write down while it is calm: your policy numbers, a room by room home inventory, what to photograph before, and the damage and claim logs that decide how much you actually get paid. Undated, so it works this season and every season after it.

WHAT IS INSIDE

Four sections, in the order a storm actually happens.

BEFORE THE SEASON, while nothing is coming
• Policies and coverage. Wind, flood, vehicles, with the deductibles written in dollars rather than percentages
• Every number you want in the first hour: outage lines, account numbers, the neighbor with a key, the out of state contact
• What to photograph, room by room, and where the files and documents have to live
• Nine room by room home inventory sheets, 24 lines each, with make, model, serial and replacement cost
• A water and supply calculator that works from your household size, your pets and the number of days

WHEN A STORM IS NAMED
• The countdown, written as a sequence: 5 to 7 days out, 72 hours, 48 hours, 24 hours, and when the wind arrives
• The shutdown sequence for the last hour in the house, in the order that matters
• What goes in the car, and the evacuation plan agreed in the spring instead of at midnight

AFTER IT PASSES
• The re-entry safety page, to read before you touch anything
• A damage log with a column for cause, because wind and flood are different policies
• The claim call log: date, time, who you spoke to, their direct number, and what they promised
• Making the claim, including the deadlines that quietly end good claims
• A contractor vetting sheet, and a page on exactly what the storm chasing scams look like

THE PART MOST CHECKLISTS LEAVE OUT

Almost every hurricane list you can find ends when the wind stops. The money does not. It is lost afterward, in the documentation, and that is where more than a third of these pages sit.

Contents coverage is usually 50 to 70 percent of what the house itself is insured for, which on most policies is a six figure number. To collect it you have to say what you owned. People routinely settle for a fraction of their contents limit because listing four hundred items from memory, in a rental, three weeks after losing them, is impossible. The inventory sheets exist so that list already exists.

WHY IT IS WRITTEN FOR THIS COAST

Federal advice says three days of supplies. Three days is for a house that was merely inconvenienced. After a Gulf hurricane that made landfall near you the realistic number is seven, and fourteen if you are rural or at the end of a long feeder line.

A flood policy takes 30 days to take effect, so June 1 is already too late to buy one. Mold is established in Gulf humidity within 24 to 48 hours, which changes what you do about the air conditioning when you leave. Carbon monoxide from generators kills more people in the days after a storm than the wind does during it. Fire ants raft in flood water and the raft looks like a patch of floating dirt.

WHO IT IS FOR

Anyone on the hurricane coast who has never done this before, anyone who has done it once and found out the hard way what they wished they had written down, and anyone who has just bought a house and does not yet know what their policy actually says.

Both open in any PDF reader, with no special software or fonts to install.

Print the whole thing, or just the pages that apply to you. Print the inventory sheets as many times as you have rooms. Interior pages are white on purpose, so printing does not cost you a cartridge.

Instant download the moment checkout completes. Nothing is mailed.

HOW YOU MAY USE IT

This is a personal use license. Print as many copies as you need for your own household, and update the fillable version as often as you like.

Please do not resell the file, share it as a file, or list it anywhere as your own. If someone wants a copy, send them the listing.

ALSO IN THE SHOP

The Gulf Coast Home Maintenance Calendar, a 27 page printable kit covering the whole year rather than the storm: twelve months of tasks, the Big Ticket Watch List of what is already on a clock in your house, and seven sections for whatever yours happens to have.

FREE CALENDARS FOR YOUR PHONE

Twelve months of Gulf Coast home maintenance are also available free as three subscribe-able calendars that drop into Google, Apple or Outlook and repeat every year. No purchase needed. Subscribe free at gulfcoasthomemaintenance.com.

If anything is wrong with your download, message me and I will fix it, and the fix reaches everyone rather than just you.

General preparedness and claims guidance, not legal advice and not a substitute for the terms of your own policy. Read your declarations page, and follow the instructions of local emergency management, which outrank anything printed here.
```

## Photos

Etsy crops the search-grid thumbnail to **4:3**, which slices the title off a
portrait page. Compose the first image at 4:3 rather than screenshotting a page.

Put **33 PAGES, FILLABLE, UNDATED** on that first image, and put the word
**HURRICANE** on it large enough to read at thumbnail size. Shoppers in a hurry
scan images before they read titles, and in this product's selling week the
buyer is in more of a hurry than usual.

Run `python build_binder_listing.py` for all ten images, into
`product/listing-binder/`. Ten is Etsy's maximum. Suggested order, which is the
numbered order the script emits:

1. The 4:3 hero
2. The cover
3. Policies and coverage, the page that proves this is not a generic checklist
4. The home inventory intro, which carries a worked example line
5. A Kitchen inventory sheet, 24 blank lines
6. The countdown, which shows the sequence idea at a glance
7. The shutdown sequence
8. The claim call log
9. Contractor vetting
10. Water and supplies

**Images 4 and 5 are a pair and the order matters.** A blank ruled sheet on its
own undersells: a shopper scrolling past it sees an empty page rather than a
product. The intro page goes first because it shows a real filled line, a make,
a model, a serial and a price, which says what the standard is. The Kitchen
sheet follows and says how much room there is to meet it.

Beyond that pair, 5 and 8 are mostly blank lines by design. Do not substitute
prose pages to make the images look busier. A buyer scrolling these needs to
understand within two images that this is a thing they fill in.

## Price

**$16.99.**

Etsy takes roughly $0.20 to list, 6.5% of the sale, and about 3% plus $0.25 to
process payment, so $16.99 nets about **$14.93**.

Why higher than the kit at 33 pages against 27: page count is not what is being
paid for. The kit is bought by somebody being responsible in January and
competes against free checklists. This is bought by somebody frightened in
September, competes against nothing that carries the claim documentation, and
sits next to a wind deductible measured in thousands. A buyer deciding whether
$16.99 is worth it has, on the same screen, just read that their own deductible
might be $8,000.

Why not higher still: it has to stay an easy decision made in one minute
without asking anyone. Past about $20 a digital printable starts getting
thought about, and thinking is the enemy of a panic purchase.

Worth considering: list at $16.99 and run a launch sale through August. Etsy
shows a strikethrough and a discount badge, which helps conversion on a listing
with no reviews yet, and ending the sale on September 1 leaves you at the real
price going into the peak without having to justify a rise.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will this work outside the Gulf Coast?**
Yes, most of it. The inventory sheets, the claim and damage logs, the
contractor vetting and the photograph method work anywhere a storm goes, or for
that matter after a fire or a burst pipe. The supply numbers, the timing and
some of the detail are written for Gulf conditions: long power restorations,
mold within 48 hours, flood policies that need 30 days.

**Is it dated?**
No. There is no year anywhere in it, so the same printout works every season.
Fill it in once and update the parts that change.

**Do I have to print it, or can I type into it?**
Either. There are two files. The fillable one has 1,876 form fields, so every
inventory line and every log row can be typed and saved. Saving works in Adobe
Reader and most desktop PDF apps; some phone apps let you type but cannot save,
so use a computer if you want to keep it.

**Do I need a color printer?**
No. Plain US Letter paper, and it is designed to read in black and white.

**How is this different from the maintenance calendar in your shop?**
The calendar is the whole year: twelve months of upkeep, what is on a clock in
your house, and what to do in which month. This is one event, in depth. Some
buyers want both and they do not overlap much.

**I already have a hurricane checklist. Why this?**
Most checklists end when the wind stops. More than a third of this is what
happens afterward: the inventory that lets you collect on contents coverage,
the damage log, the call log, the claim deadlines, and how to avoid the
contractors who arrive with the storm.

**What are the free phone calendars?**
Three subscribe-able calendars that put twelve months of Gulf Coast home
maintenance into Google, Apple or Outlook and repeat every year. Subscribe free
at gulfcoasthomemaintenance.com. They need no purchase.
