Why the Gulf Coast is its own problem

The national averages assume mild summers, dry air, and a roof that isn't being sandblasted with salt. Four things here break that math.

Salt air

Corrodes condenser coils, fasteners, and flashing years ahead of schedule.

Humidity

Drives mold, rot, and pests into crawlspaces and wall cavities you never look at.

Termites

Formosan and native subterranean colonies are endemic — and damage is almost never covered by insurance.

Hurricane season

June 1 to November 30, with a flood insurance waiting period that makes May the real deadline.

What a month looks like

May

Hurricane prep month. Season opens June 1.

Must
Verify your insurance before the season starts: wind, and flood if you carry it. Flood policies generally take 30 days to take effect. Photograph every room and the exterior for your claim file. Trim limbs back from the roof.
Should
Test the generator. Restock supplies. Check that shutters or plywood are on hand and fit.
Above
Inspect soffit vents and roof edges. Secure sheds, fencing, and anything that becomes a projectile.

Getting them onto your phone

Start with Must Do

Add the others when you're ready. The whole point of the tiers is that you don't have to do everything at once.

Subscribe rather than download

A subscription keeps updating as the calendar improves — including the how-to videos being added to each task. A downloaded file is a snapshot: it never changes again, and it duplicates itself if you import it twice.

Google Calendar

The Google Calendar app can't add a subscription itself. Do it once from a browser — phone or computer — and it appears on every device signed into that account.

  1. Tap Add to Google Calendar above.
  2. Confirm Add when Google asks.
  3. In the app, open the menu and switch the new calendar on.

Google refreshes subscribed calendars slowly, sometimes up to a day. Nothing here is urgent to the hour, so it doesn't matter. A newly added calendar may show its web address as the name until Google's first refresh — that's normal, and it corrects itself.

Apple Calendar or Outlook

Use Subscribe, which hands off to your default calendar app.
