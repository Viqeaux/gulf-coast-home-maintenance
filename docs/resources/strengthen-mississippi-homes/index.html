Mississippi's $10,000 FORTIFIED roof grant, and where to check if you qualify

The state of Mississippi will pay up to $10,000 toward re-roofing your house to a standard built for hurricanes. The program is called Strengthen Mississippi Homes. This page is a map to the people who run it, not a substitute for them.

Checked against the Mississippi Insurance Department's own pages, and against Senate Bill 2409, on October 1, 2026

Eligibility and status change between phases, so treat the department's page as the answer and this one as the directions to it. Everything below says where each fact comes from, and every rule that decides whether you get the money lives on their site, not this one.

What the program is

Strengthen Mississippi Homes is a grant program run by the Mississippi Insurance Department. Their page says it covers up to $10,000 of the cost of upgrading a roof to the FORTIFIED Home™ Roof standard, paid directly to the contractor when the work is finished rather than to you. Anything the roof costs above the award is yours, and so is the evaluator's fee, which the grant does not cover.

It is not only a hurricane program any more, and it is no longer temporary. Senate Bill 2409 became law in the 2026 session. It renamed the old Comprehensive Hurricane Damage Mitigation Program, set the award at $10,000 per home in statute, widened the damage it covers to hurricane, tornado, hail and other catastrophic windstorm events, and removed the repealer that had given the program an expiry date. It also left the department free to set who can apply by regulation, which is why the rules below are a phase rather than a law, and why they can move without anything being passed. Awards still depend on the legislature appropriating the money.

The program page, the application, and every current rule are here:

  • Strengthen Mississippi Homes, on mid.ms.gov, the page that decides everything on this one.
  • Questions go to SMH@mid.ms.gov, the address the department publishes for the program.

Who the program is for, and who the current round is for

These are two different questions and the department answers them in two different places, which is where the confusion on this starts.

The program itself is statewide. Their September 2026 FAQ opens eligibility to "residents of Mississippi" with no county limit at all. There is one condition attached to geography rather than a restriction: a house in the six coastal counties, which their FAQ lists as Hancock, Harrison, Jackson, Pearl River, Stone and George, has to sit in a jurisdiction that has adopted and actively enforces a building code meeting or exceeding the FORTIFIED roof standard. That is a question about your city or county government, not about your house, and your local building office is the one who can answer it.

The round that is open is narrower. Rounds open periodically and are announced in advance, applicants are drawn by lottery, and the department's banner limits the current one to wind pool policyholders. So you can be eligible for the program and still have nothing to apply to today. That is a reason to watch the page, not a final answer.

Who qualifies in the round that is open

As of the date at the top of this page, the department's page limits the current phase to:

  • Wind pool policyholders. Applications are open to MWUA policyholders only, and a recipient must have been insured by the wind pool for three consecutive policy years before applying. Their page asks people who do not meet this not to apply.
  • The house you live in. Owner-occupied, single-family, claimed as your primary residence. Their FAQ says townhomes, rental property, condominiums and mobile homes do not qualify.
  • Insurance in force. A homeowners policy that includes wind coverage, and flood insurance as well if the house sits in a special flood hazard area. You also have to show wind coverage in force when the work is finished, not only when you apply.
  • A house in good repair, evaluated by a certified FORTIFIED evaluator. Their FAQ defines good repair as well maintained, with any damage being recent and storm-related: a roof already wrecked by a tornado, windstorm or hail does not disqualify you, ordinary neglect does. The evaluation is paid out of pocket, and the evaluator's fee is not covered by the grant.
  • One grant per household, ever.

The phrase their page uses is "at this time". Selection inside a round is by lottery, so meeting every rule above gets you into the draw rather than into the money.

Five things that cost people the grant

All of these are in the department's own FAQ, and all of them are easier to get wrong than they look.

  • You cannot do the work yourself, and you cannot use your own roofer. The contractor has to be one the program has approved and trained, licensed in Mississippi and carrying liability and workers compensation cover.
  • You cannot start early. Work done before the award letter, or outside the scope on it, is not covered. You pick from approved contractors, three of them bid, and the award letter comes after the department has reviewed those bids.
  • Applying is online only. Their FAQ is blunt that there is no paper or telephone route, which is worth knowing if the person who needs this is not the person reading it.
  • The evaluator's fee is yours whatever happens. If you walk away because the bids come in over $10,000, that money is not refunded. You can opt out, but only before you sign with a contractor, and you have to tell the program in writing within 30 days of the award letter.
  • Anything over the award is yours. The grant pays up to $10,000 and no more, so the gap between that and the winning bid is your bill.

Two things worth knowing that are not traps: the grant is a grant, not a loan, and is never repaid, and a FORTIFIED designation lasts five years before it needs a re-inspection to renew.

How to find out if this is you

  1. Pull your declarations page. The wind pool is the Mississippi Windstorm Underwriting Association, MWUA. If they carry your wind coverage, their name is on the declarations page of that policy. The association's own site is msplans.com, and it carries its own notice about this program.
  2. Read the round that is open on the department's program page. The policyholder requirement above is that page's rule for this round, and rounds change.
  3. Apply when a round is open, from that same page. Rounds are announced in advance and applicants are drawn by lottery, so applying early in a round is not worth anything but applying at all is.
  4. Email the program at SMH@mid.ms.gov if you are not sure which side of a rule you sit on. They administer it; nobody else's answer is binding.

What a FORTIFIED roof actually is

FORTIFIED is a construction and re-roofing standard from the Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety, the insurance industry's research lab, and the name is their trademark rather than the government's. Their site, fortifiedhome.org, explains the standard in homeowner terms and is where the evaluator requirement above starts to make sense: the designation is inspected onto the house, not claimed by the roofer. The department's page notes a designation lasts five years.

Where the program is headed

The law behind the program, Senate Bill 2409, passed unanimously in spring 2026, and the department's own September 2026 FAQ now says plainly what the reporting said then: the program is open to residents of Mississippi, with awards drawn by lottery rather than first come, first served. It is the rounds, not the program, that have started on the coast with wind pool policyholders. No date for a wider round is published anywhere I could verify, so this page does not print one: the department's page is where a new round will be announced, and watching it costs nothing.

Worth knowing while you are here

If you hold a wind pool policy, the number worth looking up before any storm is named is your wind deductible. On most coastal policies it is a percentage of the dwelling limit rather than a flat dollar figure, which is how a $400,000 house comes to owe $8,000 before the policy pays anything. That is your policy's own math, on your own declarations page, and knowing the dollar figure changes what a roof grant is worth to you.
